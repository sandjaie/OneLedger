ADR-0008 — Import 24 months of history

Status: Superseded by ADR-0013 · Date: 2026-09-04

This decision no longer holds. It is kept as the record of what was decided and why it changed — deleting it would leave the current architecture looking arbitrary. The decision in force is ADR-0013.

What changed: this ADR chose 24 months of history. The horizon is now 36 months, anchored at 01 January 2024. Everything below describes the system as of 04 September 2026, not the one being built.

Context

How far back should statement import go? The choice trades net-worth trend quality against parser effort, and the effort is not linear: older statements use retired layouts, each needing its own parser version.

Decision

Current year plus prior year — approximately 24 months.

Opening balances at the start of that window are entered manually as the anchor.

Rationale

24 months is the point where trends become real. It gives year-on-year comparison for every month, a full seasonal cycle twice over, and a net-worth curve with enough points to have a shape. Twelve months gives no YoY comparison at all; thirty-six adds relatively little analytically.

Parser effort rises sharply beyond it. Banks revise statement layouts every few years. Two years back usually means one layout per institution. Five years back can mean three, each needing its own parser version, its own fixture and its own maintenance — for data that is progressively less relevant to current decisions.

The FX backfill matches. 24 months of daily rates is a single API call. It aligns the rate table with the transaction horizon so there is never a transaction without a rate.

Manual opening balances are honest and cheap. Roughly fifteen numbers entered once, at the window boundary, anchoring everything derived after it.

Alternatives considered

Everything available (5+ years). Richest long-term story. Rejected because dedupe and cleanup of old, differently-formatted statements would become the bulk of Phase 1 — turning the most motivating milestones into a data-entry project, on data that mostly informs nostalgia rather than decisions.

Go-forward only. Fastest to a working app; opening balances entered and nothing historical imported. Rejected because the net-worth trend — the primary long-term metric — would take a year to become interesting, and the classification engine would have no history to learn from. Both of the product's most valuable features would be dormant for twelve months.

Consequences

Good. Phase 1 stays scoped. Parsers target current layouts only. YoY comparison works from day one. The FX backfill is bounded.

Costs. Net worth before the window start does not exist. Long-run charts begin two years ago rather than at the beginning of financial life. Accepted: the questions this product exists to answer are about the present and the future.

Extending later is straightforward — import older statements and let the pipeline handle them. The cost is parser versions for older layouts, which can be paid if and when the older data is actually wanted. Nothing about this decision forecloses it.
