OneLedger architecture decisions

ADR-0020 — Assisted ledger decisions

Status: Accepted — owner direction, 10 September 2026 · Supersedes: ADR-0017 · Clarifies: ADR-0003

Context

The owner wants a consolidated financial picture without personally classifying recurring merchants or clicking every transfer pair. The owner delegates routine ledger judgement to the assisting agent and asks to be consulted when the evidence is ambiguous. The previous handoff's owner-only link and unlink restriction is withdrawn for this workflow.

ADR-0017 correctly moved matching to committed transactions, where both legs exist. Requiring the owner personally to perform each confirmation is no longer the chosen trade-off. Financial correctness, household isolation and the distinction between observed facts and inference remain requirements.

Decision

Routine work is delegated; uncertainty is escalated. The agent may inspect the authorised working ledger, classify supported cases, create reusable merchant rules, and confirm or correct transfer links through supported application operations. The owner need not repeat that review or perform the clicks. This authority concerns ledger records, not initiating bank payments, trades or other real-world financial actions.

Matching still operates on committed transactions. Transactions remains the place to inspect and correct results, not a mandatory owner approval checkpoint. The agent checks each proposed batch before applying it and reports a concise, privacy-safe outcome.

Classification and learning

  • Use the existing deterministic cascade, merchant aliases, rule preview and classification backfill. Household-specific knowledge is database data, never institution-specific code or real statement text committed to git.
  • Owner explanations are evidence. Record a reusable, narrowly scoped rule when the explanation generalises; distinguish direction, account and narration where necessary. Preview the affected set before applying it. Preserve explicit overrides.
  • A payment rail identifies how money moved, not what was purchased. Merchant identity can support a category; a person's name alone cannot establish groceries, dining or another specific purpose. Use a broad supported category or leave it unresolved, then ask one grouped question for recurring ambiguous patterns rather than one question per row.
  • Public research may identify a provider's business, but cannot prove the purpose of every payment to it. Distinguish investment funding, subscriptions, fees, refunds and redemptions using direction, source evidence and the owner's explanation. Never manufacture fund units, a destination posting, a holding value or an asset from a debit alone.
  • Saving a rule makes later matching deterministic. Automatic learning from every correction and retroactive replacement of existing classifications are separate implementation work; the current backfill fills only unclassified rows.

Transfer judgement

Confirm a pair only when both committed legs exist, belong to the household, are unclaimed, have the correct directions on distinct accounts, and have corroborating economic evidence. For a card payment, check the bank debit against the card credit, amount, posting window and payment narration or reference. For a remittance, check destination identity and the source's stated destination amount/currency or a shared reference, with any difference supported by the statement. Inspect competing peers on both sides; a plausible alternative means ask.

A confidence score ranks candidates; it is not a calibrated probability or sufficient permission to link. Matching amounts and dates alone do not prove a transfer. A missing peer, unexplained fee, split payment or uncertain household boundary stays unresolved. Do not hide an expense by assigning a transfer type solely from a keyword.

Use the existing atomic confirmation and unlink operations and their exclusivity guards. Identify delegated review and its evidence in transfer notes. The existing match_method records candidate versus manually selected matching, not who reviewed it. Unlinking retains the rejected record and restores the legs; re-linking during acceptance must be deliberate, not automatic resurrection of a rejected pair.

Provenance, privacy and implementation boundary

Retain raw transactions, frozen amounts and source-document traceability unchanged. Keep classification rule identity and source fields; label agent-authored rules in their names. Keep the applied scope and prior derived values in private local run evidence before a classification batch, so a correction can be targeted. This is not a claim that the current schema already has a complete decision-history or one-click batch-undo feature.

Use supported authenticated, household-scoped operations; do not bypass authorisation with direct SQL writes. Public searches contain public provider names only, never raw ledger narrations, personal counterparties, identifiers or amounts. Private ledger evidence stays out of source control. The separate clean local-production database receives no working-ledger data or household-specific rules as a side effect of this work.

ADR-0003 still defers LLM calls made by the application. Owner-requested assistance in this conversation, including public provider research and operation of existing controls, is permitted now; it does not authorise a new external transaction-data feed. An embedded AI classifier would require an explicit privacy and provider decision.

No migration is needed to permit this delegated workflow. Unattended transfer confirmation, durable per-decision provenance and correction learning require a separately reviewed implementation with bounded matching policies, ambiguity handling, an activity record, correction support and regression evidence. This decision permits that direction; it does not turn a documentation change into a shipped background service.

Acceptance and alternatives

The delegated agent may execute the remaining slice 1.3 and 1.4 checks, including controlled link, unlink and re-link operations, an independent statement-based month tally, and card-dues comparisons with source PDFs. Keep the exact spending deltas, restoration, frozen-rate and net-worth assertions unchanged. Record the run as agent-executed under owner delegation, never as unaided owner acceptance. Only verified results may mark a slice Accepted.

Retain mandatory owner clicks: rejected because it imposes the routine work the owner is delegating. Confirm everything above a score: rejected because unrelated or competing legs can score highly and silently remove genuine spending. Evidence-based delegated review reuses today's application while collecting the evidence needed for reliable unattended automation.
