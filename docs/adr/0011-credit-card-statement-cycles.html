ADR-0011 — Model credit card statement cycles as entities

Status: Accepted · Date: 2026-09-04

Context

A credit card can be modelled as an ordinary liability account with a running balance. That covers "how much do I owe" and nothing else.

But a card's useful questions are all cycle-shaped: what was billed, what is the minimum, when is it due, was the last one paid in full, what is the utilisation the bureau sees.

Decision

Model cards as liability accounts plus a card_statement entity per cycle: period, opening and closing balance, statement balance, minimum due, due date, credit limit, finance charges and reward points.

Rationale

"What is due on the 15th" is not derivable from a running balance. The statement balance is a snapshot at the statement date; the current outstanding includes everything since. Paying the current outstanding when the statement balance was due is a different act with different consequences. Without the cycle object, the product cannot tell you the number you actually need on the 14th.

It makes payment matching reliable rather than fuzzy. A payment can reconcile against a known statement balance instead of being matched on amount similarity alone. Since card payment is the most common double-counting error (Invariant I4), making its detection near-certain is worth an entity.

Paid-in-full detection is a genuine signal. A card paid in full is free credit. A card carrying a revolving balance is expensive debt, often at 30%+ annually, and it should be visually distinct from the first case. That distinction only exists with cycles.

Utilisation needs the statement's limit. Limits change. The utilisation figure that matters is the one at the statement date, which is what a credit bureau sees.

The data is already in the statement being parsed. The card statement PDF states the limit, the minimum, the due date and the statement balance. Not capturing them means parsing a document and discarding most of what it says.

Alternatives considered

Running balance only. Much less to build. Rejected: no due-date tracking, no minimum due, fuzzier payment matching, and no paid-in-full signal — losing most of what makes card tracking worth doing.

Cycles plus payment reminders and risk alerts. Genuinely useful. Deferred rather than rejected: due dates are surfaced on the dashboard in M6, and proactive alerting is a small Phase 2 addition on top of the same data.

Consequences

Good. "What is due when" is answerable. Utilisation matches what a bureau sees. Payment matching becomes near-certain. Finance charges become visible and attributable — which is the number most likely to change behaviour. Reward points get tracked as a side effect.

Costs. Card statement parsing must extract the metadata block, not just the transaction table — a different extraction problem within the same document, and the metadata block's layout varies more between issuers than the transaction table does. A missing cycle leaves a gap that oneledger doctor flags, since gaps in card statement coverage are how utilisation history goes quietly wrong.
