ADR-0017 — Transfers are confirmed on the transactions screen

Status: Accepted · Date: 2026-09-10 · Supersedes: ADR-0007

Context

ADR-0007 decided that transfer candidates are detected automatically with a confidence score and “confirmed by the user in the review queue”, with high-confidence pairs pre-ticked and nothing linking without a click. Every part of that reasoning still holds. One fact about the data does not fit the location it names.

A transfer’s two legs are almost never in the same import batch. A card payment’s debit appears on the bank statement and its credit on the card statement, and those are two different documents imported at two different times. A remittance’s legs are two countries and two producers apart. The review queue is a per-batch screen: it shows one statement’s staged rows. It therefore cannot show both legs of a real transfer except by coincidence.

There is also a structural reason, independent of timing. A staged row is not yet a transaction and cannot carry a transfer_id; confirming a pair across a batch boundary would mean linking a row that does not exist in the ledger yet. Slice 1.2’s acceptance run made both concrete: 67 statements imported across seven accounts, with every card payment and every inter-account remittance split across two separate batches.

Decision

Automatic candidate detection with a confidence score, computed over committed transactions and confirmed by the owner on the transactions screen. High-confidence pairs are grouped and pre-ticked. Nothing links without a click.

The four transfer-candidacy columns V13 put on staged_transaction stay unwritten. They are not removed: once pair history exists, a staged proposal may become worth showing, and that is a later decision with evidence behind it rather than a guess made now.

Rationale

Only the location changes. ADR-0007’s decision rests on an asymmetry — a wrong silent link removes a genuine expense from spending and nobody notices, against one extra click per transfer — and that asymmetry is untouched by which screen carries the click. Scoring, pre-ticking, grouping, and the refusal to link without confirmation all survive unchanged.

The transactions screen is where both legs exist. It reads the whole ledger rather than one batch, so a candidate pair can be shown at all. It is also where the phase plan already puts manual link and unlink, so confirmation and correction live in one place instead of two.

It is superseded rather than reinterpreted. Reading “the review queue” loosely as “wherever review happens” would leave an accepted decision whose text the implementation visibly does not match, and the next reader would have to rediscover why. An ADR that is quietly reinterpreted stops being a record.

Alternatives considered

Keep confirmation in the import review screen, proposing only pairs whose peer is already committed. Honours ADR-0007 literally. Rejected: for a card payment the two statements are imported at different times, so whether a transfer is ever offered depends on import order — and on the ordering that the acceptance run actually uses, the card statement is imported after the bank statement that pays it, so the payment is never proposed at all.

Propose at staging and confirm later. Rejected: a staged row cannot carry a transfer_id, so the proposal would have to be held somewhere and replayed after commit — state with no owner, to save a step the transactions screen already provides.

Consequences

Good. Both legs of every real transfer can be shown together, which ADR-0007 intended and its location prevented. Confirmation and manual linking share one screen and one scorer. The existing 4,184 imported transactions become eligible for matching immediately, rather than only statements imported from now on.

Costs. Confirming transfers is a separate pass from reviewing an import rather than part of it. The pre-ticked grouping ADR-0007 relies on to keep that cheap has to be built on the transactions screen instead.
