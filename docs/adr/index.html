Architecture Decision Records

Each ADR records one decision, the alternatives that were genuinely considered, and the trade-off accepted. They exist so that a decision is not silently relitigated six months later by someone — including a future you — who has forgotten why it was made.

Everything numbered here is in force. Four decisions were made and replaced during the specification work, before any code existed; they are summarised in Decisions we changed and hold no number, because a folder where a third of the pages describe an abandoned architecture misleads on sight.

Changing a decision

Do not edit an accepted ADR to reflect a new decision. Write a new one that supersedes it, and mark the old one Superseded by ADR-XXXX. The reasoning that led to a decision stays useful even when the decision changes — often especially then.

That rule starts at the first commit of application code. Until then, a reversed decision was never built on and never constrained anything, so it is summarised in Decisions we changed rather than kept as a numbered page — and the number it held goes back into the sequence. Once something depends on an ADR, reversing it has a migration story, and that story is exactly what a superseding ADR is for.
