ADR-0007 — Auto-detect transfers, confirm in review

Status: Accepted · Date: 2026-09-04

Context

Transfers between accounts must be excluded from income and expense, or every spending figure is wrong. In a two-country life this is severe: a year of remittances can exceed a year of actual spending, so getting it wrong doesn't skew the number, it inverts it.

The question is how a transfer gets identified: manually, automatically, or somewhere between.

Decision

Automatic candidate detection with a confidence score, confirmed by the user in the review queue. High-confidence pairs are pre-ticked. Nothing links without a click.

Rationale

A wrong silent link is uniquely damaging. It removes a genuine expense from spending and attaches it to an unrelated transaction. Nothing errors. Nothing looks wrong. The monthly figure is simply too low, and it stays too low until someone happens to audit that month — which, for a personal tool, may be never.

Compare that with the failure mode of requiring confirmation: an extra click per transfer, on a handful of transfers per month. The asymmetry is stark.

Manual-only linking is real work. Salary transfers, card payments, remittances, SIP debits and ATM withdrawals add up to dozens of pairs monthly. Hand-linking all of them is exactly the friction that makes people stop using a tool.

Confirmation is nearly free when the matching is good. With pre-ticked high-confidence pairs grouped together, confirming a month's transfers is one keystroke, not one per pair. The cost of the safety property is close to zero.

The confidence threshold can be raised later, from data. After several months, the false-positive rate at each score band is measurable. Raising the auto-confirm threshold then is an informed decision; doing it now would be a hopeful one.

Alternatives considered

Fully automatic above ~0.90. Least friction. Rejected on the asymmetry above — the failure is silent, and silent wrongness in financial data is the thing this whole specification is organised against.

Manual only. Zero false positives. Rejected on friction: dozens of links a month, every month, forever.

Consequences

Good. No transfer is ever created without explicit confirmation. Wrong suggestions are visible and rejectable at the moment they are proposed. Detection improves over time via pair-history scoring, so confirmation gets cheaper. Confirming a remittance is also the natural moment to show what it cost in fees and spread — a genuinely useful thing to surface at exactly that point.

Costs. A click per transfer group per import. One-sided transfers stay pending until the peer statement arrives, which means a pending item that must be understood as normal rather than alarming. oneledger doctor surfaces those older than 30 days, since a long-pending transfer usually means a missing statement.

Not a permanent decision. If, after a year, high-confidence matches prove reliable, this can be revisited with actual false-positive data rather than intuition. That would be a new ADR.
