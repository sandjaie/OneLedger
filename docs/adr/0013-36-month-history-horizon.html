ADR-0013 — Import 36 months of history, anchored at 01 Jan 2024

Status: Accepted · Date: 2026-09-05 · Supersedes: ADR-0008

Context

ADR-0008 set the horizon at ~24 months on the reasoning that 24 months is where trends become real and that older statements use retired layouts, each costing a parser version.

Two things have changed since it was written.

The statements are in hand. 71 files covering Jan 2025 – Sep 2026 have been gathered, and they are not six layouts — they are four. Both credit cards share a single issuer layout, and both Indian accounts are Axis with an identical format. The "each extra year costs parser versions" premise is weaker than assumed when 0008 was written, because the layouts observed are stable across the whole period already collected.

Calendar-year reporting was chosen (Q20). A window starting 01 Jan aligns every annual figure, year-on-year comparison and opening-balance anchor to a reporting boundary rather than cutting across one.

Decision

Anchor the history window at 01 Jan 2024 — approximately 36 months.

Opening balances are entered manually as at 01 Jan 2024 and are the anchor for every derived balance.

Rationale

Two complete prior calendar years, not one. 24 months from a January anchor gives one full year plus the current partial year. 36 months gives two complete comparison years, which is the difference between "this year versus last year" and an actual trend.

XIRR needs history to mean anything. A return figure computed over 18 months of SIP contributions is dominated by the most recent NAV. Three years is where the number starts being worth acting on, and XIRR cannot be backfilled later (ADR-0006) — the data has to be there from the start or not at all.

The marginal cost is gathering, not code. The parsers are the same. FX backfill grows from ~730 to ~1,100 daily rates, which is still one API call. The ledger grows from roughly 20k to 30k rows, which is immaterial against the free tier (architecture.html §9).

Alternatives considered

Keep 24 months (01 Jan 2025). Less to gather, and it matches the statements already in hand with nothing missing. Rejected because it buys a saving in the one-off task that is already the cheapest part, at the cost of the comparison year that makes the trend readable.

01 Apr 2025, aligned to Indian FY. The default stated in questions.html Q6. Rejected once calendar-year reporting was chosen: an Apr anchor with Jan–Dec reporting means the first reporting year is permanently incomplete.

Five years. Rejected. Statement availability degrades — most banks expose 24–36 months through online channels — and layouts genuinely do drift that far back, which is 0008's argument still holding at a longer range.

Consequences

  • Statements for Jan–Dec 2024 must still be gathered. The 71 files in hand start at Jan 2025. This is a prerequisite for M2 being meaningful, and it is the user's task, not the code's.
  • Where a 2024 statement proves unobtainable, that account's opening balance is anchored at the earliest date actually available and the gap is recorded — visibly, per Invariant I7 — rather than silently starting the account later.
  • FX backfill range extends to 01 Jan 2024.
  • oneledger doctor coverage checks run over 36 months, so a missing month is reported for the full window rather than only the recent one.
