Personal Finance OS — Product Coverage and Requirements

Accepted product direction: 6 September 2026. OneLedger is in Phase 0. This document incorporates the product review; requirements below are planned capabilities, not claims about working software. The Superpowers progress file records delivery status. Phase 0 retains its current scope.

The product must answer five questions: What do I have? Where did my money go? What is coming next? Am I on track? What needs my attention? The ledger provides the trusted history. Commitments, goals, resilience and follow-up make that history useful in decisions.

2. Financial meaning — Phase 1 reporting requirements

F01 — Household boundary. The user identifies which assets and liabilities belong to the tracked household. A spouse-owned asset can be tracked under the existing single login; ownership does not grant application access. Closing an account or changing country preserves its historical ownership and reporting context.

  • Internal transfers cancel across the household; they are not income or consumption.
  • Support, gifts and other money leaving the household are external outflows. Show them separately from consumption, but include them when calculating retained income and available resources.
  • A missing peer statement does not establish that a transfer is external. Keep it pending and show the incomplete coverage until the user confirms its meaning.
  • Refunds reduce the relevant spending category on their posted date; they do not become salary or other earned income. Link the original purchase where known without rewriting prior periods.
  • A reimbursable purchase can create a receivable when the user confirms it. Receipt of the reimbursement settles that receivable; do not count both a receivable and new income. Uncertain claims remain expected receipts, clearly distinguished from recognised assets.

F02 — Metric definitions. Consumption spending, external household outflows, retained income, investment funding and account cash movements are distinct views. Retained income is income less net consumption spending and external household outflows. Savings rate is retained income divided by income; show “not applicable” when income is zero or negative. Investment funding is purchases less redemptions funded across the cash/portfolio boundary; exclude internal portfolio transfers and reinvested distributions. Display its ratio to income as a funding measure, which can exceed 100% when existing savings are invested. Do not label it newly earned saving.

F03 — Net-worth attribution. Internal movements do not create wealth. A purchase of 50,000 from existing cash reduces cash by 50,000 and increases investments by 50,000: net change zero before costs or price changes. Repaying 50,000 of principal from cash reduces cash and debt by equal amounts: net change zero. Contributions and principal reduction can appear in supporting allocation views, but must cancel their funding legs in consolidated attribution. The waterfall separates retained income, market returns, asset revaluations, FX effects, other identified external changes and an explicit residual. Recognised investment income appears once, not in both retained income and market appreciation.

F04 — Money in transit. Show unmatched internal-transfer amounts, source and expected destination separately from settled cash. Do not silently fabricate a destination posting. A verified-position view shows its coverage gap; any estimated economic position that includes in-transit value must show the assumption and remove it once settlement is imported. Pending, failed, reversed and settled transfers must remain distinguishable.

3. Trust my position — Phase 1

F05 — Freshness and progressive onboarding. A monthly statement establishes a position at its statement date. Every headline shows its as-of date and whether it is verified, partially covered or estimated. Price, FX and statement dates are separate: updating prices does not refresh cash balances. A forecast never presents itself as a bank-confirmed available balance.

Start with account inventory, acknowledged opening balances, and one complete recent month. Show missing accounts and periods explicitly. This is an early usability checkpoint; it does not replace the history anchor of 01 Jan 2024 or the Phase 1 history-completion gate in ADR-0011.

A basic overview lands in Phase 1.4 after transfers. It shows the verified position for included accounts, spending, external outflows, dates and missing coverage. Manual valuations can represent assets whose detailed investment import is not ready, with their age visible and no second counting when holdings arrive. Full investment analytics and the document library do not block this overview.

F06 — Investment and account lifecycle. Phase 1.6 acceptance covers dividends, reinvestment, partial sales, charges, security splits and transfers between platforms as applicable to the holdings actually owned. Moving holdings preserves acquisition history and is not a new contribution or return. Unrecognised corporate actions block the affected holding's return claim and create a review item. Phase 0.4 preserves closed accounts in historical views; changing reporting currency pair follows ADR-0002 rather than relabelling stored amounts.

4. Manage the next month — Phase 3.1 and Phase 3.2

F07 — Commitments and forecast. Start with manually entered, confirmed commitments. Each has an amount, currency, expected date, paying/receiving account, recurrence if any, and certainty (confirmed or estimated). Support income, card bills, rent, subscriptions, investment contributions, annual payments and planned transfers. Automatic recurring detection proposes entries for confirmation.

  • Show 30-, 60- and 90-day cash forecasts by account and currency, including the lowest projected balance and its date. Expected income that is uncertain must be visible as an assumption.
  • A purchase is spending when posted; its later card payment is a bank cash commitment, without counting the purchase again in spending. Allow partial payments and variable statement amounts.
  • A commitment matched to an actual payment stops contributing a second future outflow. Cancelled, overdue, partly paid and fulfilled commitments remain distinguishable.
  • A planned remittance has both funding and receipt dates, fees and an estimated rate; it identifies a funding need without moving money. Account shortages remain visible even when consolidated funds are positive.
  • Funds reserved for a goal or emergency buffer are not freely available. Show balances, commitments and reservations separately, applying each reservation once.

F08 — Needs attention. Extend the existing review and data-quality experience with one action list. Each item has a reason, linked account/document/goal, due date when applicable, and open, snoozed, completed or dismissed state. Dismissal records why; a recurring obligation's next occurrence remains independent. Resolve source-driven items when their underlying condition is fixed.

Phase 1.x surfaces existing import, coverage, card-due and valuation warnings. Phase 3.x adds follow-up for commitments, renewals, reimbursements and goal shortfalls. No automatic bank action is implied. The monthly review checks coverage, resolves transfers and classification, compares the position, reviews upcoming commitments and records follow-up. Target: import plus ledger review under 20 minutes; measure planning time separately rather than hiding new work inside that target.

5. Build resilience — Phase 3.3 and Phase 3.4

F09 — Emergency reserve. The existing liquid-net-worth toggle remains a broad user preference. Emergency funds are a separate selection of accessible resources, with access delays, restrictions and existing commitments visible. The user chooses an essential-spend baseline, its reference period, and a reserve target in months. Show eligible uncommitted resources divided by that baseline as estimated coverage; zero or unknown expenditure yields “not enough information,” not infinite runway. Show each currency's resources and conversion assumptions.

F10 — Goals. Each goal has a target, target currency, date, priority, linked resources, current earmarking, planned contributions and a shortfall/surplus. The same resources cannot be reserved twice across goals and the emergency reserve. Earmarking is a plan, not a ledger transaction. Show contribution progress separately from market/FX changes. Releasing a reservation changes available resources without changing wealth. Simple target/date planning comes first; retirement, inflation and return scenarios require explicit user assumptions and belong to later analysis.

F11 — Protection and administration. A small register links to the existing vault:

RecordInformation and action
InsuranceCovered person/asset, provider, coverage amount/currency, premium, renewal date, policy document and last-reviewed date; no automated adequacy recommendation
DepositMaturity date, expected proceeds, renewal instruction and certificate; maturity converts an asset to cash rather than creating the principal as income
Claim or reimbursementCounterparty, expected amount/currency, submission date, evidence, received amounts and outstanding status; partial settlement supported
AdministrationUser-entered deadlines, required documents and completion status; no inferred legal deadline or filing advice
Nominee detailsWhether recorded with the institution, last-reviewed date and document reference; no bank credentials or full identity numbers

Tax readiness means organised records and exports. Jurisdiction-specific calculations stay in Phase 5.6; tax filing remains excluded. These registers require no AI.

6. Portability and continuity

F12 — Owner export. Phase 1.8 provides a downloadable owner copy of transactions, accounts and available holdings in open tabular form, plus selected original documents and a manifest of coverage/as-of dates. Currency, sign, relationships and provenance remain interpretable. Require the same authorization as viewing the records; omit secrets and protect temporary exports like source documents. This is distinct from advisor sharing or collaboration, which remain excluded.

Emergency handover is a recognised gap, not an approved access feature. Revisit separately with an explicit privacy and authorization decision if a trusted-person workflow becomes necessary. The current one-user boundary remains in force. Similarly, schedule loans, property-specific workflows and advanced retirement modelling only when the household needs them.

For real data, reconcile each included native account balance to its evidence and identify every remaining discrepancy. Valuation uncertainty and incomplete coverage are separate labels, not a blanket 1% allowance for errors. A synthetic complete scenario has zero unexplained residual.

Success is knowing the verified position and next obligations, identifying uncommitted resources, explaining wealth changes, progressing toward goals and maintaining the monthly routine. Daily opening frequency is useful feedback, not proof of success on its own.
