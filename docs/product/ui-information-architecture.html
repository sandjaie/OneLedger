11 — UI and Information Architecture

Two modes, with genuinely different requirements:

  • Daily — ten seconds, glanceable, answers "am I fine?" without interaction.
  • Monthly — twenty minutes, dense, gets a month of statements in.

Optimising for one at the expense of the other is how these tools fail. The dashboard is deliberately sparse; the review queue is deliberately dense.

1. Navigation

Phase 1, matching the brief:

Overview          the morning screen
Accounts          what you own and owe
Transactions      the ledger
Cash Flow         income vs spending
Investments       portfolio and allocation
Loans             debt and amortisation
Net Worth         the long-term metric
Documents         the vault
─────────────
Review            appears with a badge when a batch awaits review
Settings          categories, rules, institutions, FX, backup

Phase 2 adds Budgets, Recurring and Salary. V2 adds Ask.

Persistent header: the currency toggle (the household's pair, INR ⇄ AED in the mockups below) and a date-range control on analytical screens. The currency toggle is global, persisted, and instant — it swaps a column, it does not recompute (see multi-currency-and-fx.html §2).

Design rules

  • Every headline figure carries a delta, because a number without a direction is not information.
  • Both currencies shown for net worth; the toggle sets which is primary.
  • Attention items at the bottom, and only when they exist. An always-present alerts region trains you to ignore it.
  • No chart above the fold except the sparkline. Charts invite study; this screen is for glancing.
  • Data-quality warnings appear here when they affect a headline figure — a stale valuation, a month with no imported statement. A number the system doesn't trust should not be presented as though it does (Invariant I7).

3. Review — the screen that decides whether this gets used

Density and speed. This is where twenty minutes a month are spent, and the screen that decides whether the tool survives a monthly routine.

Principles

  • Grouped by attention needed, not chronologically. Chronological order means scrolling past 183 correct rows to reach 11 that need work.
  • Reconciliation banner at the top. Green means the parse is verified; red blocks import entirely (Invariant I8).
  • Bulk actions over per-row clicks. Reviewing 200 rows one at a time is why people abandon these tools. High-confidence rows arrive pre-ticked and collapsed; the work is confirming groups, not visiting rows. Target: 200 rows reviewed in under 10 minutes.
  • Fully operable by keyboard — Tab, Enter and Space reach every control. This is an accessibility requirement (WCAG AA, stated in prd.html §6), not a power-user feature. There are no vim-style bindings.
  • Transfers show their cost at the moment of confirmation — the most informative place to see what a remittance actually cost.
  • Correct rows collapse. They are shown as a count, expandable, not as 183 lines to scroll.
  • Anomaly hints where an amount is out of pattern for a merchant.
  • Categorizing offers rule creation inline, with an affected count (classification.html §4).

│                                                                          │
│  03 Sep  TT REMITTANCE TO INDIA        ⇄ Transfer → HDFC NRO             │
│          ENBD Current · UAE                −AED 5,000.00  ₹111,000  🔗   │
│                                                                          │
│  05 Sep  SALARY SEP 2026               Income → Salary                   │
│          ENBD Current · UAE               +AED 26,000.00  +₹6,22,440     │
└──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
  • Original currency first, converted second. Never the conversion alone.
  • ⓘ reveals the FX detail: rate, date, and whether it was carried forward.
  • 🔗 marks a transfer leg, linking to its peer. Transfer rows are visually distinct because they are not spending.
  • Filters: account, date, category, merchant, amount range, currency, country, type, tag, review state, source document.
  • Bulk select for recategorization, tagging and exclusion.
  • Each row links to its source document at the correct page.

  • Attribution is the point, not the line chart. The chart shows that it changed; this shows why.
  • FX translation is explained in words, not just shown as a bar. It is the least intuitive component and the one most likely to be mistrusted.
  • The residual is displayed with a probable cause (Invariant I7). Hiding it would discard the best data-quality signal the system has.

6. Other Phase 1 screens

Accounts — grouped by class, then country. Each row: name, institution, native balance, converted balance, last-imported date. A staleness indicator where no statement has been imported recently, because an account with old data quietly distorts net worth.

Cash Flow — monthly income/spending/net with a 12-month trend; category breakdown drilling through to transactions; the Need/Want/Investment/Debt split; savings rate and investment rate shown separately.

Investments — allocation across the seven asset classes as the primary view, holdings table below it. Per holding: invested, current, absolute return, XIRR, and price as-of date. Allocation before individual funds, always.

Loans — per loan: outstanding, EMI, rate, remaining tenure, payoff date, and a principal-vs-interest chart over the life of the loan. That chart is genuinely educational the first time you see it.

Documents — filterable library; each document shows how many transactions it produced and links to them.

Settings — categories (with bucket assignment), rules (with hit counts and health warnings), accounts, institutions, statement layouts, FX rate inspection, backup status, data-quality dashboard.

Statement layouts is where the product's agnosticism is visible (ADR-0016). Each saved layout shows its column mapping, how many documents it has parsed, and when it was last used. A layout can be re-mapped when a bank changes format — which increments its version, leaving already-imported rows pointing at the version that produced them.

The mapping UI itself opens from the import screen when a file matches no saved profile:

7. Cross-cutting

Money formatting

Currency Format Large values
INR ₹1,36,42,180 — Indian grouping ₹1.36 Cr, ₹42.10 L
AED AED 5,982,530.00 AED 5.98 M

Indian grouping (2,2,3) and lakh/crore abbreviation are used because they are how the numbers are actually read. Abbreviations appear in headline figures; exact values in tables and always on hover.

Sign presentation

  • Expenses and liabilities: minus sign and colour. Never colour alone — it fails for colour-blind users and in print.
  • Liability screens show what is owed as a positive ("You owe AED 4,500"); the database keeps it negative (data-model.html §1).

Empty, loading, error

  • Empty states teach. An accounts screen with no accounts explains what an account is here and offers the first one, rather than showing a blank table.
  • Loading: skeletons matching final layout, never spinners on financial figures. A spinner where a number belongs reads as an error.
  • Errors are specific. "Reconciliation failed: declared 12,450.00, computed 12,140.00, divergence at row 47" — never "Something went wrong".

Responsive

Desktop-first for import and review; those are genuinely desktop tasks. The dashboard, accounts, transactions and net worth are fully usable on a phone, because that is where the daily ten-second check happens.

Accessibility

Keyboard navigation throughout, and complete in the review queue — every control reachable by Tab and operable by Enter or Space, with a visible focus ring. WCAG AA contrast. Semantic tables. Screen-reader labels including currency and sign — "minus 31 dirhams, Starbucks Dubai Mall".

8. Visual approach

Restrained. Financial data is dense and the interface should recede.

  • Neutral base, with colour reserved for meaning: positive, negative, warning, transfer.
  • Tabular figures for every number, so digits align in columns.
  • Density over whitespace in tables; whitespace in the dashboard.
  • Charts: line for trends, bar for comparisons, waterfall for attribution. No pie charts — allocation is a stacked bar or a treemap, both of which support comparison in a way a pie does not.
  • Dark and light, both fully supported and both explicitly designed rather than one derived from the other. This gets opened early in the morning and late at night.
