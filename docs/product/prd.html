01 — Product Requirements

Product: OneLedger Owner: single user (household-capable) Last product review: 6 September 2026; finance OS coverage and requirements accepted

1. Problem

A financial life split across two countries is invisible in aggregate.

Each institution shows only a partial picture. The bank where the salary lands knows about the current account. The bank in the other country knows about the savings account. The broker knows about equity. Nobody knows the total, and nobody can answer the questions that actually matter:

  • Am I getting wealthier, and why?
  • What did sending money home actually cost me last year?
  • What proportion of what I earn ends up invested rather than spent?
  • What is committed before I have spent anything discretionary this month?

The first deployment is a UAE + India household, and the worked examples throughout these documents are drawn from it. Nothing in the design is: the countries, the currencies, the institutions and the statement layouts are all rows in tables.

Existing tools fail for a specific reason: they are built for one country. They treat a second currency as a display conversion rather than a first-class property of the data, and they treat an international remittance as a large expense followed by a large income — which is wrong twice over.

2. What this product is

A single normalized ledger of every financial event, in every country, with the consolidated views that only become possible once that ledger exists.

Self-hosted, and fed by statements rather than by an aggregator — the banks that matter in this situation are usually the ones with no API and no aggregator coverage, and financial history is the last thing worth renting from a third party.

The design bet is that once every event is normalized into one well-shaped table, nearly every feature becomes a query rather than a subsystem. Net worth is a query. Cash flow is a query. Savings rate is a query. That is why the ledger and the ingestion pipeline get the majority of Phase 1's effort, and the dashboards get comparatively little.

The product also needs to answer what is coming next, whether the household is on track, and what needs attention. The finance OS requirements define cash planning, resilience, goals, protection, owner export and the meaning of headline metrics. They extend Phase 1 and Phase 2 without adding work to Phase 0.

What it is not

  • Not a budgeting app. Budgets exist (Phase 2) but are a comparison view, not the centre.
  • Not multi-tenant. No signup, no organizations, no public registration, no invitations. This removes an entire class of vulnerability rather than mitigating it.
  • Not a bank aggregator. No credentials are stored, ever. Statement import is the mechanism, by design and not as a temporary measure.
  • Not an accounting system. Single-entry with explicit transfer links, not double-entry journals. The rigour that matters is captured by the invariants in §4.
  • Not AI-first. No LLM calls exist in Phase 1 or Phase 2. See ADR-0003.

3. Users and context

One user. Tax-resident considerations in two jurisdictions. Earns primarily in AED, accumulates long-term wealth primarily in INR, and moves money between the two regularly.

The schema carries household_id and owner_person_id from day one so a second person can be added later without a migration that touches every table — but no multi-user behaviour is built now. See ADR-0004.

Usage shape: a reliable monthly import and ledger review under twenty minutes. Daily visits show dated positions, upcoming card dues and actions; they do not imply live bank data. Phase 2 adds a planning review, measured separately, for commitments, goals and follow-up. Every headline distinguishes verified, partially covered and estimated information.

4. Product invariants

These are requirements, not implementation notes. Each is enforced by a test (testing-and-data-quality.html) and violating one is a bug of the highest severity, because each produces silently wrong numbers rather than errors.

# Invariant What breaks without it
I1 The original amount and currency are written once and never modified Historical reports change under you
I2 Every balance is signed so that net_worth = SUM(balances) Sign confusion in liability handling
I3 A transaction linked to a transfer is never income or expense Remittances counted as ₹1L of spending
I4 A credit card payment is a transfer, not an expense Every card purchase counted twice
I5 Flows translate at transaction-date rate; balances at as-of-date rate Last March's spending changes daily
I6 Every imported transaction links to its source document and row No audit trail; can't resolve disputes
I7 Derived, carried-forward or interpolated values are labelled as such False precision presented as fact
I8 Import is rejected if declared closing balance ≠ opening + Σ rows Dropped rows go unnoticed forever

5. Modules

Each module below states its purpose, its Phase 1 scope, what is explicitly deferred, and its acceptance criteria. Phase assignments are summarised in roadmap.html.

5.1 Accounts — Phase 1

Everything is an Account. One universal model rather than separate systems per institution or per country. This is the decision that makes the consolidated view possible.

Account types

Cash & deposit    cash · current · savings · salary · fixed_deposit · recurring_deposit
Credit            credit_card
Borrowing         personal_loan · home_loan · auto_loan · education_loan · gold_loan
Investment        brokerage · mutual_fund_folio · retirement (PPF/EPF/NPS)
Illiquid          property · precious_metal · other_asset
Other             crypto · receivable · payable

Attributes that make it global, rather than there being a UAE model and an India model: country, currency, institution, account_subtype (NRE / NRO / PPF / EPF), and tax_residency_context.

Phase 1 scope

  • CRUD on accounts, with institution and country.
  • Native currency per account. An account has exactly one currency.
  • Opening balance with an opening-balance date — the anchor for all derived balances.
  • include_in_net_worth toggle (for tracking something without it counting).
  • Complete account, IBAN or card identifiers supplied by a source are stored in the owner's local database for exact statement matching. Ordinary views are masked; an explicit owner action reveals a complete stored value. A source-masked identifier remains mask-only. See security-and-privacy.html.
  • Active/closed lifecycle. Closed accounts keep their history and drop out of current views.

Deferred: multi-currency accounts (rare, and modelable as two accounts); account groups.

Acceptance

  • Every account type above can be created and appears in the right net-worth bucket.
  • An account's computed balance equals opening_balance + Σ(transactions) exactly, to 4 decimal places, for both an asset account and a liability account.

5.2 Transaction Ledger — Phase 1

The centre of the product. Everything imported becomes a row here, and nearly every other feature is a query over it.

Fields — full schema in data-model.html.

identity      booked_date · value_date · description_raw · description_clean · reference_raw
money         amount · currency  (immutable)
              fx_rate_to_base · fx_rate_to_secondary · amount_base · amount_secondary  (frozen at booked_date)
meaning       txn_type · category · merchant · counterparty
context       account · country · payment_method
links         transfer_id · card_statement_id · investment_txn_id · loan_id · recurring_series_id
provenance    source · source_document_id · source_row_number · import_batch_id · dedupe_hash
review        classified_by · classification_confidence · is_reviewed
user          notes · tags · is_excluded

Sign convention (I2): negative = money left the account, positive = money entered. This holds for liability accounts too — a card purchase is negative (you owe more), a card payment is positive. balance = opening + Σ(amount) therefore works unmodified for every account type.

txn_type is what separates a spending figure from a movement figure:

income · expense · fee · interest · tax · refund          → counted in cash flow
transfer_in · transfer_out                                 → never counted
investment_buy · investment_sell · investment_income       → portfolio, not spending
loan_disbursement · loan_repayment                         → balance sheet, not spending
adjustment · opening_balance                               → excluded from everything

Phase 1 scope

  • List with filter by account, date range, category, merchant, amount range, currency, country, type, tag, review state, and free-text search over description.
  • Inline edit of category, merchant, type, notes, tags. Editing a category offers to create a rule (§5.5).
  • Bulk select → recategorize, tag, exclude, mark reviewed.
  • Split a transaction into multiple categories (one ₹8,000 supermarket bill → ₹6,500 groceries, ₹1,500 household).
  • Manual entry, for cash spending and anything without a statement.
  • Attachment of a receipt image to a transaction.
  • Every transaction shows its source: which document, which page, which row.

Deferred: merge of transactions; recurring-transaction templating (Phase 2).

Acceptance

  • The agreed history back to 01 Jan 2024 imports and reconciles; one complete recent month is an earlier usability checkpoint.
  • Filter combinations return in under 300 ms at ~20,000 rows.
  • Every row can be traced to a source document, or is explicitly marked source = manual.

5.3 Statement Importing — Phase 1, and the hardest part

Bank APIs are deliberately not used. Statement import is the mechanism, permanently.

Pipeline

Upload → identify institution → extract → normalize → detect duplicates
       → classify → detect transfers → reconcile → user review → import

Full specification: statement-ingestion.html.

Formats: PDF (including password-protected) and manual entry. No institution in use provides CSV or Excel (Q7), so PDF extraction is the only import path, not the fallback one. Email attachment ingestion is Phase 2.

No institution is named in application code (ADR-0012). A statement layout is a row the user creates through a mapping UI on first import of an unrecognised format: the app finds the header row, proposes column bands from its x-positions, and the user confirms or drags. One generic engine reads any profile.

This is what makes the product agnostic in the way that matters: a bank account opened next year, or an old statement from an account closed years ago, needs no code change and no deploy.

Four layouts cover the seven accounts held today. They are the first four profiles created, not a fixed list:

Layout characteristics Accounts covered
Credit-card statement, bilingual, CR suffix, sign inverted 2 cards
Current account, running balance only, reverse-chronological 1 current account
Savings, declares OPENING BALANCE, separate debit/credit columns 2 savings accounts
Savings, encrypted, extracts without spaces 2 savings accounts

Parsers are versioned. Banks change layouts; a parser records which version produced a row so a layout change is diagnosable rather than mysterious.

Non-negotiables

  • The original file is stored permanently and linked to every row it produced (I6).
  • SHA-256 checksum on every document prevents re-importing the same statement (§5.11).
  • Balance reconciliation (I8). If the statement declares an opening and closing balance, the imported rows must bridge them exactly. A mismatch blocks the import and reports the delta. This single check catches the majority of parser bugs.
  • Nothing is imported without review in Phase 1. Confidence scores pre-sort the queue and high-confidence rows are pre-ticked, but the human presses import.

Acceptance

  • Each of the four gathered statement layouts imports a real statement end-to-end with zero manual correction of amounts or dates.
  • Re-uploading an identical file imports zero rows and says why.
  • Uploading an overlapping statement (Aug–Sep after Jul–Aug) imports only September.
  • A deliberately corrupted row fails the batch rather than importing silently.

5.4 Multi-Currency — Phase 1

Full specification: multi-currency-and-fx.html. Summarised here because it is a product requirement, not only a technical one.

  • INR is the reporting base. AED is a toggle. Both are stored frozen per transaction, so neither is "the derived one" and the toggle is instantaneous.
  • The original currency is always visible. AED 1,000 → ₹24,xxx at 24.15 on 03 Sep 2026, never just the converted figure.
  • Flows use the transaction-date rate, frozen. Balances use the as-of-date rate, live.
  • FX translation gain/loss is a first-class line item in net-worth attribution. When AED strengthens, INR net worth rises without any action — the product says so explicitly rather than burying it.
  • The actual achieved rate beats any reference rate. A remittance stores its implied rate from the two leg amounts; the gap to mid-market is a recorded cost (§5.6).

5.5 Classification — Phase 1 (deterministic only)

Full specification: classification.html.

A deterministic cascade. No AI in Phase 1 or Phase 2 (ADR-0003).

1. Explicit user override on this transaction   → stop
2. User rule (highest priority first)           → stop
3. Merchant alias match                         → stop
4. Recurring series match                       → stop
5. Prior classification of an identical description on the same account
6. Seed rule (ships with the product)
7. Unclassified → Transactions → Needs a category

Category taxonomy: a curated two-level tree shipping with the product so reports work on the first import. The tree is universal — Housing, Utilities, Groceries, Transport, Health, Education; the merchants that fill it arrive as rule packs, one global plus one per country the household banks in (school fees, remittance and SIP land in the tree the same way DEWA, Salik and Talabat do). The user adds, renames and merges freely.

Every category carries a bucket: need | want | investment | debt_service | income | ignore. This gives the Needs/Wants/Investments/Debt view for free from a single classification pass, rather than asking for the same judgement twice.

Learning is explicit, not implicit. Choosing a category for an unclassified row offers a private rule, showing both how many ledger rows match and how many still need a category before anything is written; the owner chooses “Always mark rows like this” or “Just this one”. Re-categorizing a row that already has an answer stays one-off until Phase 2's learn-from-correction flow. Nothing changes behaviour behind the owner's back.

Acceptance

  • Seed rules classify ≥ 70% of a first real import without any user rules existing.
  • After one month of corrections, ≥ 90% of the next import is auto-classified.
  • Every classified transaction can show which rule classified it.

5.6 Transfers — Phase 1, first-class

Full specification: transfers-and-reconciliation.html.

The single most important correctness feature in a two-country ledger.

AED 5,000 leaves Emirates NBD
        ↓  remittance
₹111,000 arrives at HDFC NRO

Two ledger rows, one movement of money, zero expense. Without this, annual spending is overstated by the entire amount remitted — potentially by more than actual spending.

Transfer kinds: internal same-currency, cross-border remittance, credit card payment, loan repayment, investment funding, cash withdrawal.

Detection: automatic candidate matching on date proximity, amount (FX-aware), direction and account pair, scored for confidence and confirmed by the user in the review queue. High confidence pre-ticks; nothing links silently (ADR-0007).

The FX cost model is what makes this more than bookkeeping. A confirmed transfer stores:

source_amount · destination_amount
implied_rate   = destination_amount / source_amount     ← what you actually got
market_rate    = mid-market on transfer_date            ← what it was worth
fx_spread_cost = source_amount × (1 − implied/market)   ← what the spread cost you
explicit_fee   = the fee the provider itemised
total_cost     = spread + fee                           ← the real cost

Which yields the report: "You moved AED 180,000 to India in 2026. It cost you AED 3,240 — AED 1,100 in stated fees and AED 2,140 in exchange-rate spread." That number is invisible in every bank statement and is one of the more genuinely useful things this product can produce.

Acceptance

  • Salary → savings, card payment, remittance and SIP debit are all detected as transfers.
  • Confirmed transfers are absent from every spending figure.
  • Annual remittance cost report reconciles to the sum of individual transfer costs.
  • No transfer is created without explicit user confirmation.

5.7 Credit Cards — Phase 1

Cards are modelled as liability accounts plus first-class statement cycles (ADR-0009).

Per card: current outstanding, credit limit, utilisation %, statement balance, statement date, payment due date, minimum due, and whether the last statement was paid in full.

Statement cycle object — each imported card statement becomes a record with period, opening and closing balance, statement balance, minimum due, due date, limit, finance charges and reward points. This is what makes "what is actually due on the 15th" answerable, and what makes payment matching reliable: a payment can reconcile against a known statement balance rather than a fuzzy amount.

Payment = transfer (I4). Enforced. The expense was recorded at purchase; counting the payment would double it.

Acceptance

  • Utilisation matches the card statement.
  • A payment appears in neither spending nor income.
  • A card paid in full shows differently from one carrying a revolving balance.
  • Total card spending in a cycle equals the statement's total debits.

5.8 Investments — Phase 1, transaction-level

Tracked at the level of individual lots — units and NAV per transaction — not as snapshots (ADR-0005). XIRR cannot be backfilled later without re-entering everything, so it is built now.

Per holding: units, average cost, invested amount, current value, absolute return, XIRR, asset class, fund category, country, currency, platform, account holder.

Instrument coverage, Phase 1: Indian mutual funds (AMFI scheme code, NAV), Indian equities, US/international equities and ETFs, gold (ETF, SGB and physical), FDs and RDs, PPF/EPF/NPS.

Asset allocation is the headline view, not the list of individual funds:

Indian Equity · International Equity · Debt · Gold · Cash · Real Estate · Other

Twenty individual funds is data. The allocation is information.

Valuation: market instruments use fetched prices where a free source exists (AMFI daily NAV file for Indian MFs); everything else uses manual valuation snapshots (§5.9). Stale prices are labelled with their as-of date (I7).

Acceptance

  • XIRR for a known SIP series matches a spreadsheet XIRR() to within 0.01%.
  • Allocation percentages sum to 100% and reconcile to total portfolio value.
  • A holding in USD contributes correctly to both INR and AED views.
  • Buying a fund shows the bank debit and the investment purchase as one linked event, not two.

5.9 Manual Valuations — Phase 1

For assets with no importable statement: property, physical gold, EPF, PPF, anything opaque (ADR-0006).

The user records "as of 30 Sep 2026, this is worth X". Net worth uses the most recent snapshot at or before the date being viewed — carried forward flat, never extrapolated.

Staleness is shown, not hidden (I7). A property valued 14 months ago is displayed with its age. Net worth including significantly stale valuations says so.

Acceptance

  • Net worth on any past date uses the snapshot in effect on that date, not the latest one.
  • Snapshots older than a configurable threshold surface a prompt to update.

5.10 Loans — conditional, descheduled pending Q18

Loans are proper entities with amortisation, not just negative balances.

Per loan: original principal, outstanding principal, interest rate (fixed or floating with benchmark and spread), EMI, start date, end date, remaining tenure, interest paid to date, principal paid to date, prepayments.

Amortisation schedule generated from the terms and regenerated on any rate change, prepayment or EMI revision. Each actual EMI transaction links to its scheduled installment, so the actual principal/interest split is known — which is what "interest paid this year" needs.

Deferred to Phase 2: the what-if simulator ("if I prepay AED 50k: new payoff date, interest saved, liquidity after"). The schedule engine built in Phase 1 is what makes it a small addition later rather than a rewrite.

Acceptance

  • Generated schedule matches the lender's amortisation table to within rounding.
  • A recorded prepayment regenerates the schedule and shows the revised payoff date.
  • Interest paid in a period reconciles to the sum of the interest components of actual EMIs.

5.11 Documents Vault — Phase 1

Since documents are being imported anyway, the product becomes the financial document repository.

Types: bank statements, card statements, payslips, loan statements, investment statements, CAS, tax documents, insurance policies, property documents, FD certificates.

Every document carries: institution, type, period, currency, account, uploaded date, and SHA-256 checksum. The checksum is what makes duplicate imports impossible rather than merely unlikely.

Bidirectional traceability: from a document, see every transaction it produced; from a transaction, open the document at the right page.

Acceptance

  • Re-uploading the same file is detected by checksum before parsing begins.
  • Every document is retrievable and openable.
  • Deleting a document is blocked while transactions reference it.

5.12 Cash Flow — Phase 1

Monthly income vs spending, with the transfer exclusion (I3) applied throughout.

  • Monthly income, spending, net, and 12-month trend.
  • Savings rate = (income − net consumption spending − external household outflows) ÷ income. External support is shown separately from consumption. Zero or negative income shows “not applicable.”
  • Investment funding rate = net cash funding of investments ÷ income; excludes reinvestment and platform transfers. It can exceed 100% when existing savings are invested and is not labelled new saving. See metric definitions.
  • Category breakdown, drill-through to transactions.
  • Need / Want / Investment / Debt split from category buckets (§5.5).
  • Both currencies via the toggle.

Acceptance

  • Spending for a month equals the sum of its reportable transactions, exactly.
  • Removing a transfer link changes the figure; adding one restores it. (Proves I3 is live.)

5.13 Net Worth — Phase 1, the primary long-term metric

Assets                          Liabilities
├ Cash                          ├ Personal loans
├ Investments                   ├ Home loans
├ Real estate                   └ Credit card debt
└ Other assets
                Net Worth = Assets − Liabilities

Liquid net worth follows the user-selected account toggle (Q22). It does not mean uncommitted cash or an emergency reserve. Those are separate Phase 2 measures with access restrictions and earmarking visible.

Attribution is the feature. A trend line that goes up is much less useful than one that explains itself:

Sep 2026 → Sep 2027          ₹1.02 Cr → ₹1.36 Cr        +₹34.0 L

  + ₹27.0 L   retained income
  + ₹ 8.0 L   market appreciation
  + ₹ 1.4 L   FX translation gain (AED strengthened)
  − ₹ 2.0 L   asset depreciation
  − ₹ 0.4 L   unexplained

The decomposition method and the FX/flow split are specified in multi-currency-and-fx.html §7. The residual is shown, not hidden (I7) — a decomposition that doesn't reconcile is telling you something.

Acceptance

  • Net worth on any date equals the sum of signed account balances on that date, in both currencies (I2).
  • Attribution components plus residual equal the actual change, exactly.
  • Complete synthetic scenarios have zero unexplained residual. Real discrepancies are identified individually; valuation uncertainty and incomplete coverage are labelled separately.

5.14 Budgets — Phase 2

Actual vs expected. Deliberately not the centre of the product.

                 Budget     Actual      Δ
Housing           5,000      5,000       —
Transport         1,500      1,280    −220
Dining            1,200      1,640    +440
Shopping          1,000      1,420    +420
Subscriptions       500        460     −40

Plus the coarser and often more useful Need / Want / Investment / Debt view.

5.15 Recurring Expenses — Phase 4.1

Automatic discovery of subscriptions, rent, school fees, insurance, SIPs, EMIs, telecom and software from repeating amount/merchant/cadence patterns.

Output: "Your committed recurring expenses for October are AED X before any discretionary spending." Plus detection of price increases and of subscriptions that quietly kept charging.

5.16 Salary Intelligence — Phase 4.2

Payslip extraction into line items rather than fixed columns — UAE and Indian payslips differ too much for a fixed schema. Separates annual compensation (including employer contributions and accrued bonus) from actual cash received. Yields bonus history, increment history and compensation trend.

Finance OS additions — Phase 3.x and Phase 4.x

Phase 3.1 and Phase 3.2 deliver confirmed commitments, a 30–90-day cash forecast by account and currency, and actions with due dates and resolution states. Phase 3.3 and Phase 3.4 deliver emergency-reserve coverage, goals with exclusive earmarking, and a register for insurance, maturities, claims and administration.

Budgets, recurring detection, salary and investment analysis remain planned. Simple commitments can be entered before their automatic discovery exists. Phase 1.8 includes an owner export; tax readiness and document organisation do not depend on Phase 5.6 tax calculations.

5.17 Ask My Finances — Phase 5.2

Natural-language questions answered by generating a SQL query against the normalized ledger, not by reading statements. The normalized ledger built in Phase 1 is what makes this feasible; attempting it earlier would mean an LLM reading raw documents, which is both expensive and unreliable. Requires the privacy decision deferred in ADR-0003.

6. Non-functional requirements

Area Requirement

Devices Desktop-first for import and review; mobile-readable dashboard

7. Success criteria

Phase 1 has succeeded when:

  1. History across all four gathered layouts is imported and reconciled back to 01 Jan 2024 (ADR-0011).
  2. The user can identify the verified position, missing coverage, upcoming card dues and required follow-up from the overview.
  3. Monthly import + review takes under 20 minutes.
  4. Net worth is trusted enough to be used in an actual financial decision.
  5. Infrastructure spend stays at zero while local, and is a single predictable line item once hosted.

Criterion 4 is the real one. A financial dashboard that isn't trusted gets checked twice against the bank and then abandoned. Everything in §4 exists to earn that trust.

8. Risks

Risk Impact Mitigation
PDF parsing is harder than expected High One PDF layout end-to-end in Phase 1.1, followed by the other supported layouts in Phase 1.2; reconciliation blocks faulty imports
Bank changes statement layout Medium Versioned parsers; reconciliation (I8) catches it as a failure, not silent corruption
Outgrowing the instance Medium Disk growth is the only thing that accumulates, at roughly 40 MB a year. §9 of architecture.html sizes it
VPS outage or loss Medium Nightly backups copied off the box; a tested restore. The stack rebuilds from one compose file
Scope creep into AI High Locked out by ADR-0003. Not a judgement call during the build
Transfer detection produces false links High Review queue is mandatory; nothing links silently (ADR-0007)
Manual valuations decay Low Staleness indicators (I7); prompts on age
Motivation lost mid-build High Phase ordering is deliberately front-loaded for usefulness — Phase 1.1 makes the tool usable before the complete overview

9. Out of scope, permanently

Recorded so they don't get relitigated:

  • Bank credential storage or screen-scraping. Never.
  • Multi-tenancy, signup flows, organizations, billing.
  • Mobile native apps (the web app is responsive).
  • Tax filing. Tax reporting begins at Phase 5.6; filing is never.
  • Trading, brokerage integration, or order placement.
  • Sharing, collaboration or export-to-advisor features. Owner downloads remain in scope; emergency handover requires a separate access/privacy decision.
